Blood pressure

A phone camera cannot measure blood pressure accurately. Apps that claim to are not reliable, so this app does not guess it. Use a cuff BP machine (about ₹1,500 to ₹2,500) and log the reading here. The app tells you which range it falls in and keeps your history.

Thermal camera view

Phones have no heat sensor, so this needs a plug-in USB-C thermal camera. Blue or purple patches on walls are cold spots, usually damp, a leak or a draft.

Some thermal cameras only work in their own app. If so, use it and tick "Cold spots" on the scan page.

Spirit box test (explained)

Spirit boxes jump between radio stations to make choppy static. This plays the same kind of sweeping static for 30 seconds. Note any "words" you hear, then read why.

Did you hear words?

Most people do. This static is random and computer-made, with no radio and no message in it. The brain is built to find speech in noise and fills in words, more so when it expects them. This is called auditory pareidolia. A spirit box does the same with scraps of radio, which is why it seems to "answer". It cannot pick up spirits.

About the readings

Phone sensors are not lab instruments. Sound is an approximate level from an uncalibrated microphone; phones cannot record true infrasound (below 20 Hz). Light from the camera is rough because the camera adjusts itself. EMF needs a magnetometer the browser can read; in Chrome on Android, if it shows "Not available", open chrome://flags, turn on "Generic Sensor Extra Classes" and reload. All data stays on your phone.
