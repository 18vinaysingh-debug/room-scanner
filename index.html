Thermal camera view

Phones have no heat sensor, so this needs a plug-in thermal camera. Plug it in, tap the button and pick it from the list. Look for blue (cold) patches on walls, windows and near pipes: they usually mean damp, a leak or a draft.

Some thermal cameras only work in their own app. If yours shows nothing here, use its app and tick "cold spots" in the room checklist.

Spirit box test (explained)

Ghost hunters' spirit boxes jump between radio stations, making choppy static. This plays the same kind of sweeping static for 30 seconds. Listen and note any "words" you hear, then read why it happens.

Did you hear words or a voice?

Most people do. The static here is completely random and computer-made, with no radio and no message in it. The brain is built to find speech in noise, so it fills in words, especially when you expect to hear them. This is called auditory pareidolia. A real spirit box does the same with fragments of radio stations, which is why it seems to "answer". It cannot pick up spirits.

About the readings: phone sensors are not lab instruments. Sound is an approximate level from an uncalibrated microphone, and phones cannot record true infrasound (below 20 Hz). Light from the camera is rough because the camera adjusts itself. The EMF test needs a phone magnetometer that the browser can read, mainly Chrome on Android. No device, app or gadget can detect ghosts, souls or "negative energy"; this app checks the physical causes that are known to make people feel uneasy.
